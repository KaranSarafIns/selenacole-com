What a Shoot Day Actually Looks Like

2026-10-25

People imagine a shoot day is all wind machines and someone feeding me grapes. The truth is more beautiful and much funnier: it starts at 6 a.m. with me negotiating with an alarm clock, and it ends with 400 photos of which eleven will ever see daylight.

The middle is choreography in every sense. A location that looks like a dream and has no bathroom. A golden-hour window exactly nine minutes long. Hair that behaves until the camera appears, then files for independence. In Barcelona we shot at the Sagrada Família at sunset and I changed outfits behind a strategically held coat while a tour group applauded what they assumed was street theatre.

And the eyeliner. There is always an eyeliner incident. We do not speak of the eyeliner incident. We simply budget for it.

Why tell you this? Because the glamour is real, but it is built — by a team, on a schedule, with snacks. When you see the twelve perfect seconds, I want you to also see the six happy hours underneath them. That's the whole show. — S.
